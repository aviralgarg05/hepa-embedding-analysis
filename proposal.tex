{\large\bfseries Making HEPA's bound empirical:}\\[1pt]
{\large\bfseries an \emph{a priori} test for whether HEPA will work on a new dataset}\\[2pt]
{\footnotesize Aviral Garg \;$\cdot$\; Forgis research proposal \;$\cdot$\; 10 October 2026 \;$\cdot$\;
on HEPA (Petersen et al., arXiv:2605.11130v4)}
\vspace{2pt}\hrule\vspace{3pt}

\section{The gap I want to close, stated by the paper itself}

Proposition 1: $I(H_t;E) \ge I(H^*;E) - C_\eta L^2 \varepsilon$, with vacuity threshold (eq.~17)
$\varepsilon_{\text{vac}} = I(H^*;E)/(C_\eta L^2)$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \B{Within a dataset it is validated:} varying $\varepsilon$ via pretraining snapshots gives Spearman
      $\rho = -0.87$ / $-0.67$ / $-0.64$ / $-0.49$ on C-MAPSS-1 / C-MAPSS-3 / MBA / SMAP.
\item \B{Across datasets it falls apart.} Pooling converged $\varepsilon$ over the 14 benchmarks gives Pearson
      \hl{$r = -0.05$ ($p=0.90$)}. The paper attributes this to $L$, $C_\eta$ and target scale differing by dataset.
\item \B{The constants are never estimated.} In the authors' words: \emph{``The constants $L$ and $C_\eta$ are not
      estimated directly; fig.~3 validates only the monotonic relationship, not the full quantitative bound,''}
      and in Future Work: \emph{``deriving fully empirical versions of the information-retention bound that
      estimate $L$ and $C_\eta$ directly from data remains an interesting open problem.''}
\end{itemize}

\hl{As it stands the bound only explains HEPA's failures after the fact.} I want to make it predictive.
Define the \B{dimensionless slack}
\[
\hat\rho(\mathcal{D}) \;=\; \frac{\hat\varepsilon}{\hat\varepsilon_{\text{vac}}}
\;=\; \frac{\hat\varepsilon \cdot \hat C_\eta \hat L^2}{\hat I(H^*;E)} ,
\qquad\text{bound non-vacuous}\iff \hat\rho < 1 .
\]

\section{Hypotheses}

\begin{itemize}
\item \hl{H1 (normalisation restores the cross-dataset signal).} Across the 14 benchmarks,
      $\rho_s(\hat\rho,\ \text{h-AUROC}) \le -0.6$, where raw $\varepsilon$ gives $r=-0.05$.
      \emph{Falsified if $|\rho_s| < 0.3$}, which would mean the incommensurability comes from somewhere else
      and my normalisation is wrong.
      \B{The objection I have to design against:} $\hat I(H^*;E)$ comes from a probe fitted on the same labels
      h-AUROC uses, so part of any correlation could be the probe predicting itself. Control: report the partial
      correlation of $\hat\rho$ with h-AUROC \emph{given} the probe's own AUROC, on disjoint rows.
      \hl{If it vanishes there, H1 is dead and I would say so.}
\item \hl{H2 (it works as a screen).} \emph{A prediction: I have not computed $\hat\rho$ anywhere yet.}
      $\hat\rho \ge 1$ should mark where the bound gives no guarantee. Pre-registered: \B{CHB-MIT}, excluded at
      h-AUROC 0.497 with $I(H^*;E)\approx 0$ (A.6), lands at $\hat\rho \ge 1$; C-MAPSS-1 (0.806) at
      $\hat\rho \ll 1$.
      \B{CHB-MIT alone is a weak test:} the paper already reports $I(H^*;E)\approx 0$ there, so $\hat\rho \ge 1$
      is near-arithmetic. The real test is the \emph{ordering} over all 14, i.e. H1.
      \emph{H2 falsified if either control is misclassified, or if the threshold needs per-dataset tuning.} \B{What it does not mean:} appendix A.4 says a vacuous bound
      does not imply $I(H_t;E)=0$, and GECCO proves it: within-dataset $\rho=+0.14$ ($p=0.67$), yet HEPA
      scores \B{0.88} there, among its strongest table-1 results. The screen is about the \emph{guarantee}, never
      a prediction that HEPA will fail.
\item \hl{H3 (the L1/L2 gap costs more than monotonicity).} A2 is in L2 while eq.~(2) trains L1.
      \B{Appendix A.2 already answers this} via $\|u\|_2\le\|u\|_1$, calling L1 a ``monotone proxy for
      $\varepsilon$'', which is enough for the within-dataset test in fig.~3 but not here, since $\hat\rho \ge 1$
      needs $\varepsilon$ on its scale rather than its ordering. Prediction: $\hat\rho$ from the measured L2
      residual beats the L1 version by $\Delta\rho_s \ge 0.15$. \emph{Falsified if they sit within noise},
      which would license the L1 loss.
\end{itemize}

\section{Estimators, all cheap and low-dimensional where it matters}

Fit HEPA's own event head $\hat\eta(h)=\sigma(w^\top \mathrm{LN}(h) + b)$ (LayerNorm $+$ linear, 769 params on
$d{=}256$, their appendix C) on the \emph{frozen} target representations $h^*$. Then, on held-out data:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\hat L = \|\gamma \odot w\|_2/(4\hat s_{\min})$, with $\gamma$ the LayerNorm gain and $\hat s_{\min}$
      the smallest held-out $\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(h^*)+\epsilon}$. \B{The LayerNorm Jacobian is easy to drop and
      costly to drop:} $\nabla_h\hat\eta = \sigma'(z)s^{-1}P(\gamma\odot w)$, so $\|w\|_2/4$ is neither exact nor
      an upper bound. On the unit sphere at $d{=}256$, $s\approx 1/16$, so omitting it understates $L$ by
      ${\sim}16\times$ and $\hat\rho$ by ${\sim}250\times$, toward calling a vacuous bound non-vacuous.
\item $\hat C_\eta = \big(2\,\underline{\eta}\,(1-\overline{\eta})\big)^{-1}$ with $\underline{\eta},\overline{\eta}$
      the 1st/99th percentiles of $\hat\eta(h^*)$.
\item $\hat I(H^*;E) = H(\pi_e) - \mathbb{E}\big[H(\hat\eta(H^*))\big]$, plug-in. $E$ is binary, so the entropy
      is 1-D and no high-dimensional MI estimator is needed. \B{The difficulty moves rather than vanishes:} this
      is a lower bound on $I$ only as far as $\hat\eta$ fits the true posterior, so I would report it against a
      higher-capacity $\hat\eta$ as a sensitivity check.
\item $\hat\varepsilon = \mathbb{E}\|\hat h - h^*\|_2^2$ on a held-out split, and in L1 as well, per H3.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why it is worth doing, what it costs, and where it can fail}

\begin{itemize}
\item Makes HEPA's central theorem computable and falsifiable, answering the paper's own open problem; and
      $\hat\rho$ needs only unlabelled pretraining plus a small probe, so the guarantee can be checked on a new
      plant before committing. \B{Cost:} a re-run of HEPA's own recipe (under a minute per dataset on one A10G)
      plus $O(N)$ estimators, no new model.
\item \B{Risks.} Plug-in MI biases $\hat I$ down, pushing $\hat\rho$ \emph{up} toward the threshold; rank
      correlation protects H1 but \B{H2 cannot hide behind that}, so I would calibrate the threshold on held-out
      folds. And $n{=}14$ is low power: pre-register direction and test, and show the per-dataset scatter.
\end{itemize}

\section{Secondary direction (from my analysis of \texttt{embeddings.npz})}

In the attached report, with a \B{frozen} target no SIGReg weight $\alpha\in[0.01,0.9]$ bought both prediction
quality and representation rank (at $\alpha{=}0.1$: cosine $0.8716{\pm}0.0004$$\to$$0.0609{\pm}0.0044$, 5 seeds). HEPA's target is never
frozen: weight-shared, and robust to EMA per \B{appendix I.3}, which picks joint training ``for its simplicity'',
so it can move toward what SIGReg wants. That regime sits outside both settings I.3 tests.
\hl{SIGReg is doing two jobs: preventing collapse, and setting the target's distribution.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \B{H4.} Split the jobs: SIGReg on the target branch, an explicit rank floor on the predictor output.
      Prediction: h-AUROC goes \B{flat in $\alpha$} over $[0.03,0.3]$ (range $\le 0.02$), mediated by
      predictor-output effective rank. \emph{Falsified if h-AUROC still varies by more than 0.02, or if the rank
      floor binds without moving it.} \B{Not} a claim about the BATADAL/MBA gap, which the paper scopes to
      tokenisation.
\end{itemize}

{\footnotesize\emph{Worth confirming:} the paper reports $\alpha{=}0.1$ (table 7) while
\texttt{Forgis-Labs/HEPA} PR \#3 says the table-1 runs used 0.04. I've worked from 0.1.}
